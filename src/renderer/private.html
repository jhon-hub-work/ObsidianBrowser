You're browsing privately

This window runs on a throwaway profile. Obsidian doesn't know who you are here — no account, no workspaces, no history. Close the window and everything in it is gone.

What this window does
  • Keeps no history Pages you open here are never written to your history or your Core Memory.
  • Uses a separate identity Its own cookies, logins, and cache — isolated from your normal profile, and discarded on close.
  • Forgets on close Private windows are never saved and never restored when Obsidian reopens.
  • Still blocks trackers Ad and tracker filtering stays on here, exactly as it is in a normal window.
What it does not do
  • It doesn't make you anonymous Your employer, your school, your network administrator, and your internet provider can still see where you go.
  • Sites still know it's a visit Any site you sign in to knows exactly who you are, private window or not.
  • Downloads outlive the window Files you save stay on your computer after this window closes.

Why this mode exists. Obsidian is built to accumulate context — it remembers your work so you don't have to. That's the whole point of it, and it's exactly wrong for some sessions: a client's account, a second login, a search you'd rather not have shape everything that comes after. Private windows are the deliberate off-switch. Nothing observed here becomes knowledge.
